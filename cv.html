CV & Research

Where I'm Coming From

I ended up here as a child that was obsessed with codes and ciphers. My childhood bedroom is littered with ciphers I made up to share notes with my friends or encrypt my most secret thoughts. I knew what my goal was: I was going to be a spy. Unfortunately, it turns out being a spy requires a set of skills and moral opinions I turned out to not have, but the trap was set.

I became interested in computer science through a different path -- the wizardry of being able to tell a computer to do what you wanted. I made minecraft mods with my friends, and text-based games in Python. I was obsessed with Lovelace and Babbage; by this point, I was aware of Enigma and analogue crypto schemes that had been broken, but knew machine cryptography only as the magic of RSA and PGP that seemed perfect and impenetrable to me, and I yearned for the time when cryptography was a no man's land fertile for new exploration

In secondary school I ended up in the world of cybersecurity. I attended a number of weeks-long courses and workshops on all manner of related topics. There are few things cooler to a teenager than being allowed to try and break absolutely anything. Tricking fellow attendees with rubber duckies, hacking in to the old Windows 7 machines we were using, tracking down people's exact locations through public data. It was a utopia of being able to question anything, try anything -- and be wrong sometimes, too. They were really influential experiences that shaped my aims for the future.

On the topic of cybersecurity, I also had a brief stint working in cybercrime investigation and response with Netcraft. I started there weeks before Covid hit and shut everything down, thus ending the stint again. Lucky timing, eh.

I took the International Baccalaureate in my last two years of secondary school, studying SL English Literature, Computer Science and German and HL Economics, Mathematics and Physics. I will evangelize the IB possibly forever, I loved the opportunity to take a wide variety of classes, and the IB "core" was an excellent way to further develop. Theory of Knowledge encourages students to question on a deep level how we obtain knowledge in the first place, and how we trust what we know. And for my Extended Essay I could choose any topic at all, which allowed me to spend months doing my own research on the threat of quantum computers to modern-day cryptography. There's a lot of emphasis on your own research in the IB in general, actually. I still think fondly on my maths coursework project, where I investigated the phenomenon of the rising fastball.

So came the time to make a choice. What would I study at university and where? I've told a rather biased version of my background with hindsight, at the time I was genuinely unsure of whether I should study maths, computer science, or economics. I had a strong interest in law, too, but decided I could always do law conversion later, as continued to be my plan for many years. In the end, I rejected my other options and chose to attend the University of Birmingham where I completed a degree apprenticeship in computer science. DAs are awesome opportunities, in which you are employed by a sponsoring company throughout your degree, who cover university expense and pay you a salary, all while providing the opportunity to gain real-world experience working for them through your summers and an industrial year.

I graduated in the summer of 2025 with a First-class degree. Through my first two years I took a breadth of modules in CS, and ended up focusing in my final year on what ended up being my passions of security and PL. I took advanced classes in cryptography, functional programming, security of real-world systems, and programming language principles, design and implementation.

My final year project was completed with the support of Rishiraj Bhattacharyya. I looked at the key-correlated attack security of MACs from a mostly theoretical perspective. It was a really valuable experience as a window in to the world of research. It also taught me that while I loved the world of theory, moving forwards I wanted to work on an area with more direct links to application.

In the past year, I also did some work with Francois Dupressoir at Bristol University. It was really valuable to get a look inside formal verification for cryptography, and get more perspectives on research.


Where I'm Going

Currently I'm really enjoying working at the intersection of security and data systems, looking at private information retrieval that is efficient and usable. As more and more is lifted out of users' hands and in to the cloud it becomes ever more important to research methods of maintaining control, dignity and privacy.
